Site 22 caveat — the "yes" is not from the owner

The mayor stated on the record that for Site 22, the person he spoke with is "the person who has a contract on that property … not the actual property owner." The willingness to sell was expressed by the contract holder (understood to be the prospective residential developer), not the owner of record. A public commenter later asked whether the actual owner will be consulted before the site advances to city council; per committee practice, public-comment questions were not answered.

Committee Role Discussion — Hand Off to Council?

The mayor noted the original resolution asked the committee for no fewer than three potential sites, that two councilors have said they want a willing seller, and suggested the four sites could be studied for cost by the chair, the consultant, and the utilities director and taken directly to council. Tom Clemmons agreed, arguing the committee's charge is essentially complete: "we can rate those sites as a committee but pass that on … they should be evaluated based on cost feasibility and then those should go directly into council to make that final decision." Other members preferred that the committee still rank the final four (without cost) before handing off. The committee proceeded on that basis.

Engineer's Updated Information on the Final Four

Steve Jones distributed an updated packet (with 11×17 maps) adding new information for the four willing-seller sites. A recurring new element: a state law effective July 1, 2026 gives property adjacent to a sewer main the right to request service from that main — likely a reference to HB 2385 / SB 2057, which amends T.C.A. § 7-82-710(a)(1) to grant that right to property with an existing gravity sewer line on or adjacent to it. The maps therefore show, for each candidate site, the acreage that would become adjacent to the repurposed effluent force mains and thus gain connection rights. Jones noted the law is ambiguous about the level of service that can be demanded (one house vs. a 500-home development) and expects clarification in the next legislative session. Members also noted annexation implications, since several affected areas lie outside the urban growth boundary.

Cost Criterion Removed from the Scoring Matrix — 5–1–1

The committee debated whether it could meaningfully score the "cost" criterion (item 2) of its adopted matrix when land cost, site development/grading cost, and off-site road improvement costs are all unknown — only the pipe-run estimates exist. The chair argued cost is one of the most important factors but that members cannot responsibly guess it; others noted the known pipe costs alone range from ~$1M to ~$24.9M across the four sites. A motion to remove the cost criterion from the matrix passed on a roll call: Baines aye, Santana aye, Vance aye, Clemmons aye, Morehead aye; Burdine no; Thorne abstained. A revised matrix (dated July 21) will be issued without the cost item.

Observation

The committee's final ranking of the four candidate sites will now exclude cost entirely — at the same meeting where the engineer presented hard conveyance estimates differing by a factor of ~25 between sites ($1M for Site 2 vs. $24.9M for Site 22). Two public commenters challenged this: one suggested splitting the known "hard" costs (pipe estimates) from the unknown variable costs rather than discarding cost altogether. Cost evaluation now passes entirely to city staff and council, outside the committee's scoring process.

Field Narrowed to Four; Scoring Timeline

  • Motion passed to eliminate all sites except the final four: 2, 7, 13, and 22.
  • Members will independently score the four sites on the revised (cost-free) matrix and return sealed envelopes to Regina Santana's office by Wednesday, July 29.
  • Next committee meeting: Tuesday, August 4, 2026, 4:00 p.m., where the combined rankings will be revealed.
  • It was clarified that city council is not expected to make any siting decision on August 4; work sessions are anticipated first. Further engineering (site layouts, rough grading estimates) on the four finalists is expected.

Public Comment Highlights

  • Derek Dodson — Offered to provide "very good ballpark" land costs for Sites 13 and 22. Argued the Camp property on Carver Lane (whose owner objected at the last meeting) would logically become buffer or parkland, not part of a plant. Advocated Site 22: with the ~164-acre Donnell property to the north and ~100 acres to the south, he envisions up to 414 acres of future city parkland surrounding the facility, and asserted Site 22's land cost would undercut Site 13's.
  • Chad Connley (Carver Lane) — Criticized cutting the list from 12 to 4 based solely on owners' willingness to negotiate, and eliminating two-thirds of candidates without considering cost. Asked who actually provided the Site 22 "yes" (presumably the developer) and whether the owner of record will be consulted before the site goes to council.
  • Anthony Murphy (2019 Mann Road) — Offered to personally drive committee members down Mann Road, stating parts of the road measure 17 feet dirt-to-dirt (with photos), and asked that the need for road construction be on the record. Also objected to removing cost wholesale: the committee should score the hard costs the engineer has already produced and defer only the genuinely unknown variable costs.
  • Eddie Callus (family owns ~100 acres on Maple Hill Road) — Responding to his property being invoked as potential parkland/buffer for Site 22: "there's nothing that will happen to our property that would be anything but harmed by locating the sewer plant down there. I'm opposed to it from every aspect." This directly contradicts the characterization of his land as a natural complement to Site 22.
  • Stuart Maynard (1203 Balent Trace Blvd) — Asked who bears the cost of county-road improvements if a county-road site is chosen. Answer from the dais: the city — it would be part of the project cost.
