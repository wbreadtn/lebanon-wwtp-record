Site Selection Committee Meeting — June 29, 2026

Lebanon WWTP Site Selection — Public Record Archive  |  Return to main page

Video recording (YouTube)  —  Meeting began 4:00 p.m., City of Lebanon Administration Building

Scoring Results

The committee presented individual member scores for all 20 sites. Site 13 (Rockwood / Hartman Drive, understood to include the combined 13 + 13A parcels of ~140 acres) ranked first by a wide margin — approximately 10–12 points above all other sites. Committee members broadly agreed it is the clear front runner. Site 14 (Permapipe, ~24 acres) ranked second despite falling below the 60-acre threshold, scoring well on infrastructure and access.

Eliminations — Sites Scoring 32.7 and Below

The committee voted to remove all sites scoring 32.7 or below from further consideration. Sites eliminated: 6, 9, 11, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21, and 15 (by the original motion, though 15 was later reconsidered — see below).

Site 22 Added

A new site was introduced: 409 Carver Lane (Maple Hill Road area), 150 acres, inside city limits. The developer reached out proactively and indicated willingness to discuss a sale. Key characteristics noted by the engineer: no flood plain, generally flat terrain (~25-ft grade across the site), approximately 25,000–30,000 feet of conveyance required. A motion to add Site 22 to the shortlist passed.

Amendment to Re-Add Sites 15 and 17 — Failed

A motion was made to add Sites 15 (old TRW, city-owned) and 17 (city utility site) back to the list on the basis that city-owned property should not be dismissed prematurely. The amendment failed. The committee majority felt the 11-site list plus Site 22 was sufficient to advance.

Final Shortlist Vote — 7 to 3

The committee voted 7–3 to forward 12 sites to the mayor's office to identify willing sellers: sites 13, 14, 2, 7, 5, 16, 1, 8, 3, 12, 10, and 22. The three dissenting votes (Camille Burdon, Caleb Dorn, Dave Dixon) argued that the committee had committed to additional due-diligence before forwarding any list, and that willing-seller status alone should not be the deciding factor.

Second-Round Evaluation Matrix Adopted

The committee adopted a revised scoring matrix for the next phase, with six criteria (a proposed "Fatal Flaw" category was removed as too broad):

  • Community impact
  • Cost of construction, land, and operation
  • Environmental impact and permitting
  • Existing infrastructure (roads, utilities)
  • Land acquisition / willing seller (amended to include "economic return / relocation" potential)
  • Site size

The engineer (Steve Jones) noted that cost estimates depend on technology chosen, and that smaller sites constrain treatment options and future expandability. The committee directed him to provide cost estimates based on whichever process fits each site's footprint, including 20 MGD phase-one capacity and expansion potential.

Permapipe Relocation Idea

Committee member Joey Carmack proposed exploring whether Permapipe (Site 14) could be relocated to Watertown with economic development assistance, freeing the Permapipe site for the city. Caleb Dorn (also JECDB chairman) volunteered to engage the mayor's office and economic development staff on this. A public commenter later noted that Permapipe already sits on a rail line and ships exclusively by truck, casting doubt on the rail-advantage argument for a Watertown relocation.

Public Comment Highlights

  • Terry Monahan — Biofilm reactor technology halves the required footprint (~30 acres vs. 60), costs 13–15% more upfront but 50–60% less annually to operate. Argued the existing site could be upgraded in place using modular biofilm technology, eliminating the need to acquire new property.
  • Derek Dodson — Changed his preferred site from 13 to 22 based on undisclosed cost documentation suggesting Site 22 is more acres for less money. Described surrounding parcels (~100 acres and ~164 acres) as owned by parties unwilling to develop, forming natural buffers; envisioned a combined 414-acre future city park with the plant at the center. Stated Site 22 is currently for sale and has not yet exchanged hands.
  • Teresa Rogers — Asked the committee to commission an independent economic impact analysis comparing long-term taxpayer costs of each site, including effects on surrounding property values, tax base, and total life-cycle costs. Argued a willing seller does not automatically equal the best financial outcome for the public.
  • Todd Roark — Challenged the "willing seller" framing: a seller receives a premium and leaves the area, while neighboring property owners bear ongoing impacts on quality of life and property value without compensation. Finding a willing seller does not settle the public's interest.
  • Christy Chastain — Relayed that the Site 3 owner is explicitly not a willing seller and asked to go on record. Also stated Site 5 is not for sale. Questioned whether sunshine law requires public disclosure of willing-seller status as sites advance.
  • James Camp, 412 Carver Lane — Owns 14 acres at 404 Carver Lane adjacent to the Rockwood parcel (Site 13). Stated he is not a willing seller.
